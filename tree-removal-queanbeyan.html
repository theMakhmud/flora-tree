NSW Property Rules

What If the Problem Tree Is on a Neighbouring Property?

A tree that overhangs from a neighbouring property, or is rooted in a neighbour's land, raises a separate set of requirements. Under QPRC guidance, work on a tree that stands on someone else's property involves an additional step before any approval or work can proceed.

QPRC Requirement — Neighbouring Trees

QPRC says approval from the owner or manager of that land is required before seeking relevant council approvals or starting work on a neighbouring tree.

This means that if the tree is not on your property, the first step is to seek permission from the owner or manager of the land where the tree stands — before approaching council or engaging a contractor to begin work.

Note: Check current QPRC requirements before scheduling any work on a tree that may stand on land other than your own. Regulations and guidance can change; verifying the current position with QPRC is always the safest starting point.
